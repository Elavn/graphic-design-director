# Typography: rhythm, character, hierarchy, craft

**Conceptual influences:** Robert Bringhurst, Jost Hochuli, Ellen Lupton, Matthew Butterick. These are original design rules, not book quotations.

## Strategy
- Type is both language and image. Select type to suit the tone of the message, the reading context and the underlying brand.
- Build clear roles: `display / headline / subhead / body / microcopy`. Fewer clear roles beat a dozen arbitrary sizes.
- Prefer intentional contrast (weight, optical size, scale, style) over random font variety. One family with appropriate weights may outperform two families.
- Display type may be expressive; essential information must remain readable. Treat distorted or outlined text as a visual accent, not the sole vehicle for time, place or legal copy.

## Reading quality
- For long-form body copy, start around 45–75 characters per line, then adjust for typeface, column and audience; don't force this range on short poster copy.
- Start body leading around 1.25–1.5× font size for normal passages and then inspect real glyphs; tight display leading can work when carefully checked.
- Keep paragraph spacing related to leading rather than a randomly larger gap. Use either first-line indentation or space-between for body paragraphs as appropriate.
- Avoid widows, orphans, distracting rivers and unintentional hyphen ladders. Edit line breaks in headlines manually for meaning and shape.
- Evaluate tracking optically: all-caps utility labels may benefit from moderate positive tracking; lowercase body copy typically does not. Tighten or loosen display type only after viewing actual letter pairs.
- Use typographic punctuation correctly: quotation marks, apostrophes, en/em dashes, nonbreaking relationships in dates or units, and consistent numbering.

## Contemporary type directions
- **Refined editorial:** high-contrast display serif plus restrained sans body; large margins and short headlines.
- **Swiss/utility:** precise grotesk, strong grid, disciplined weight and scale changes, compact factual labels.
- **Playful expressive:** one custom-looking display role contrasted with sober, readable details.
- **Tech-forward:** variable-width or monospaced utility language with a legible humanist body; avoid `cyber` clichés.
- **Culture-led:** locally appropriate lettering and scripts, verified spelling and diacritics; never reduce cultural expression to decorative stereotypes.

## For each design, specify
- Font families or safe categories, hierarchy of weights, approximate size ratios, alignment, headline line breaks, interline spacing and label treatment.
- Read the design at actual mobile or print size, not only zoomed in.

## Reject and repair
- **Reject** five font styles of equal prominence. **Repair:** collapse to two or three clear roles.
- **Reject** a line ending with a lonely short word in an oversized title. **Repair:** rebalance the break by phrase.
- **Reject** pale thin type on an image. **Repair:** create a contrast field or adjust placement and weight.
- **Reject** artificial tracking used to make body text look “luxury.” **Repair:** keep body spacing natural; use measured tracking only on short labels.

## “What these books would reject” heuristic
Beautiful-looking letterforms are not enough when reading rhythm, spacing and line breaks are neglected. Inspect what happens **between** the letters and lines.
