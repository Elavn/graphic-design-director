# Posters, campaigns, visual wit and the single idea

**Conceptual influences:** Richard Hollis, Beryl McAlhone and David Stuart, Bruno Munari.

## Concept method
- Write the viewer's desired response: notice / understand / remember / act.
- Name the **single idea** that connects headline, visual and message. Design as an argument, not a collection of ornaments.
- Develop three metaphor candidates: literal but striking, unexpected juxtaposition, typography-as-image. Choose the strongest truthful one.
- Arrange the reading order for the environment: fast-glance poster, close-reading flyer, or in-feed thumbnail.
- Give the most urgent facts enough clarity and contrast; playfulness belongs in the hook, not in unreadable dates or venue info.
- Aim for surprise **with immediate comprehension**. If a visual pun requires a paragraph explaining it, reconsider.

## Useful conceptual devices
- **Scale reversal:** a tiny familiar object becomes unexpectedly dominant to explain the message.
- **Negative-space reveal:** one image hides a second meaning related to the purpose.
- **Semantic typography:** a word's visual treatment expresses its meaning.
- **Contrast in register:** formal type and playful image, or vice versa, produce tension.
- **Visual rhyme:** repeated forms connect two otherwise distant ideas.
- **Time-sequence:** a controlled series shows change or cause and effect.

## Modern poster energy
- Oversized confident type with modular details.
- Editorial photographic crops and tactile paper treatment, when appropriate.
- Saturated one-accent systems; carefully limited expressive type deformation.
- Controlled asymmetry, layering and contrast of analog/digital materials.
- Cultural details from research or supplied assets, not invented cultural symbols.

## Reject and repair
- **Reject** random butterflies, smoke, glows or shards on an unrelated poster. **Repair:** remove until visual supports proposition.
- **Reject** three headline ideas competing. **Repair:** one hook; subordinate remaining facts.
- **Reject** event time and venue buried at tiny size. **Repair:** create readable information module.
- **Reject** an overly abstract visual that hides the event's purpose. **Repair:** introduce one plain-language anchor.

## “What these books would reject” heuristic
Decoration is not a concept. A memorable poster offers a point of view and gets the information across.
