# Decision examples — what the skill should actually do

All situations here are **fictional or generic teaching examples**, not client instructions, official brand assets or real performance statistics.

## Example 1 — Real campaign, no brief for a website

Request: “Make a striking launch poster for a small independent cinema.”
- Interpret: poster, not UI.
- Consider ideas: projection beam becomes a city road; seats vanish into a night sky; high-contrast typographic title as cinema screen.
- Select one by audience/purpose, not by visual trend.
- Do not add bookmark icons, a fake web menu, badges and stock ratings.

## Example 2 — Reference adaptation

Request: “Use this red collage layout for my supplied green brand; replace words and products.”
- Lock major panel ratios, type hierarchy, cropping and rhythm of source.
- Replace dominant red with the client's approved primary background color; preserve red only if explicitly authorized.
- Replace logos via supplied files; verify changed words; do not invent app interface screens.
- Fail if the final design has original brand's name, wrong size or recreated approximated wordmark.

## Example 3 — Source has interface chrome

Request: “Create a standalone campaign poster like this screenshot of a portfolio site.”
- Distinguish main campaign art from navigation, bookmark, creator labels and portfolio statistics.
- Preserve relevant concept, contrast and type behavior; omit wrapper UI.
- If user explicitly says “replicate the whole screenshot,” preserve all screenshot UI within rights/feasibility.

## Example 4 — Logo-only exploration

Request: “Show three truly different wordmark directions, no mockups.”
- Explore typographic construction families, not three colored versions of the same font.
- Present all in monochrome on plain background, no vehicles, packaging or decorative symbols.
- Critique spacing/counters/scale at small sizes; do not automatically approve a first render.

## Example 5 — Text-free vehicle illustration

Request: “Flat illustrated comparison of a cargo tricycle and mini-truck, top 35% empty, no photo, 3D or gradient.”
- Produce illustration, not photoreal mockup; preserve two distinct vehicle categories and relative size.
- Keep top region truly empty; no fake text on floating price tags; flat fill and dimensional consistency.
- Fail on 3D sheen, text, logo, wrong number of wheels or forgotten negative space.

## Example 6 — Minimal conceptual advert

Request: “Illustrate resilience through a single visual metaphor, no interface.”
- Find one visual relationship that reads quickly; leave deliberate negative space.
- Avoid a cluster of metaphor stickers, quote bubbles, arrows or mechanical UI components.
- Headline should complement, not explain away, the visual surprise.

## Example 7 — Editorial event flyer

Request: “Formal exhibition opening, bold typographic, A4 print, verified copy.”
- Plan document size, bleed, margins and informational hierarchy first.
- Keep time/location readable, display type expressive where possible.
- Do not infer dates, venue, ticket cost or approvals; missing facts remain placeholders or require verification.

## Example 8 — Full moodboard requested

Request: “Show brand visual language with 4–6 relevant applications.”
- A modular presentation may be appropriate; curate one strong central artwork and a small complementary set.
- No fake KPIs, stats or unrelated app panels; display proposed logo/palette/typography truthfully.
- If existing logo is locked, do not pitch a replacement symbol in the same identity board.

## Example 9 — Local authenticity

Request: “An advertisement for a new neighborhood market delivery service.”
- Choose truthful observed or user-supplied local imagery. Avoid implying fake customers or “thousands served”.
- Connect the visual story to real customer task: goods, route, pickup, handoff, choice.
- Don't use cultural stereotypes or unverified location labels.

## Example 10 — Critique-only request

Request: “This design is too bulky and feels like an app.”
- Diagnose: density, cards, unrelated UI metaphor, lack of central concept.
- State specific deletions, recompose using purposeful hierarchy; do not silently edit if user asked only for evaluation.
- Record as medium and compositional observation, not “ban all rounded rectangles everywhere”.
