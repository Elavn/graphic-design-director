# Composition architecture — visual structure with expressive freedom

A composition is not a grid with elements sprinkled inside. It is a controlled arrangement of visual **forces**: scale, density, orientation, contrast, gaze, repetition, proximity, tension, whitespace and material depth.

## 1. Define the viewing geometry

- Canvas ratio and actual dimensions; distinguish print trim, bleed, live area and social-media UI safe zones.
- Primary focal object and focal region; direction of reading; foreground/background separation.
- Fixed anchors: logo, required copy, approved image, brand marks, legal disclaimers.
- Free regions: image crop, spatial rhythm, negative space, decorative elements only when useful.

Do not assume one aspect ratio for all tasks. Treat a requested 4:5, 1:1, 9:16, A4 or 16:9 output as an exact format constraint, not a style suggestion.

## 2. Choose composition logic

- **Single strong center:** iconic product, clear proposition, theatrical focus.
- **Asymmetric anchor:** dominant mass balanced by quiet typography or deliberate void.
- **Strict modular grid:** information systems, editorial series, brand guidelines.
- **Editorial flow:** varied column widths and image aspect ratios; pacing from loud to quiet.
- **Radial or convergent:** visual attention directed toward one event, product or character.
- **Diagonal / kinetic:** directional meaning, speed, tension; can compromise small text.
- **Framed / enclosed:** focus and clarity; not a compulsory rounded card.
- **Cropped monumental:** forces scale and physical closeness; ensure object remains legible.
- **Unstructured but controlled:** composition from gestures, collage and counterweights with a deliberate hierarchy.

A grid is a tool for repeatability, not a moral command. Break it only with compositional purpose; align elements that should relate.

## 3. Reading path and density

Draw an imaginary sequence: first ~1 second, next 2–3 seconds, then detail reading. In the first moment, one visual thought should dominate; supporting information appears in descending relevance. Protect breathing room around the strongest element; a large empty region may be the most intentional part of the design.

Avoid evenly spreading interest across every corner. “Balanced” does not mean symmetric, equally busy or full. Intentional imbalance can communicate urgency, speed, surprise and mood.

## 4. Perspective and object geometry

For product scenes, check: vanishing points, camera height, relative scale, contact shadows, occlusion, wheel positions, container/body geometry, real-world joints and plausible physical load. A striking object emerging from the ground still needs credible points of contact and light direction. For an actual product, don't replace its defining shape simply to make the composition more dramatic.

## 5. Content-image relations

Headline should either:
- provide the information the image cannot carry;
- complete a visual metaphor;
- establish necessary context or action;
- counterpoint an image intentionally.

Reject headlines that merely repeat what the viewer already sees unless repetition is the core rhetorical device. Keep long supporting copy on stable, quiet fields where possible.

## 6. Common layout failures and fixes

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everything in cards | interface pattern exported to graphic medium | choose one organizing geometry, remove unnecessary containers |
| Centered everything | no functional hierarchy or spatial story | introduce focal anchor and directional counterweight |
| Cropped subject | bad crop or wrong safe area | protect defining silhouette and intentional point of entry |
| Big void feels accidental | no visual tension across negative space | shift focal scale/crop or adjust secondary anchor |
| Typography fights image | equal contrast in competing regions | reduce one, move one, establish clear hierarchy |
| Decorative texture feels pasted on | surface unrelated to material or message | remove or derive from actual subject/technique |
| Generic polished layout | no concept, unearned symmetry | change core visual thought, not color palette |

## 7. Review in multiple states

Inspect at 100%, thumbnail, grayscale, horizontally flipped (catches imbalance), and real viewing distance. Verify protected copy and logos remain inside safe regions. A correct canvas size is not proof of correct composition.
