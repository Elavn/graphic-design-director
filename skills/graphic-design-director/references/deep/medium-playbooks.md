# Medium playbooks — art should behave like its destination

A medium is a communication constraint. Create the **thing requested**, not a mockup of that thing or a generic UI card grid. Recommendations below are default heuristics, not fixed templates.

## 1. Poster and outdoor campaign

One dominant proposition readable at glance, one hero concept or typographic act, minimal support. Test at distance and in thumbnail. Limit secondary copy; avoid website navigation, fake bookmarks, labels and creator credits unless requested. Protect bleed, crop and physical print legibility.

## 2. Social media static ad

Make the first second clear. Preserve platform-specific safe areas, ratio and actual pixel dimensions. Headline and product must survive a mobile feed. CTAs may appear as **advertising copy**, not necessarily fake tappable UI elements. Don't invent promotional offers or “verified” service claims.

## 3. Social story / reel cover

Use large, high-contrast hierarchy and upper/lower safe areas for platform overlays. Plan vertical motion/gaze. Avoid small body paragraphs and a crowded footer. If the user says “top 35% empty for text,” don't fill it in a text-free visual.

## 4. Logo and identity

Start with the mark alone, black and white, optically refined. Expand to a controlled system only after selection. Applications should test genuine scale and constraints, not distract from the logo. Don't change approved identity because a new inspiration references another brand.

## 5. Brand moodboard / presentation

Show the visual language: identity, color behavior, typography, imagery, material and a limited set of relevant applications. Decide an explicit presentation hierarchy. Modular cards are valid for **moodboards** but not a universal layout for all graphics. Never lift sample UI metrics or numbers from a reference and pretend they are client facts.

## 6. Editorial and magazine

Support reading rhythm, measure and typographic pacing. Crops can be expressive, but captions and credits must remain factual. Treat display headline, deck, body, pull quote and image as distinct roles. Avoid landing-page card widgets.

## 7. Packaging and labels

Consider die lines, folds, safe areas, material printing, ink limitations, legal labeling and shelf recognition. Never invent ingredients, compliance, warnings, sizes or contact details. Mockups are visualization, not production artwork.

## 8. Illustration / standalone artwork

If text-free, exclude all text, letter-like badges, fabricated logos and UI. Use subject and material to communicate. Honor strict visual medium: flat graphic, hand-drawn, gouache, ink, photographic, photomontage, 3D as requested.

## 9. Website and app UI

Only here are navigation tags, buttons, panels, icons, avatars, tabs and functional cards expected by default. Even for UI, justify each feature by an actual task and do not hallucinate interactions or statistics. A UI inspiration may be transformed into a *brand board* when asked, but it should never automatically contaminate a standalone poster.

## 10. Large-format vehicle wrap / signage

Work from measured, actual vehicle templates where production accuracy matters. Protect seams, hinges, lighting, reflections, handholds, door openings and safety markings. Check brand recognition from multiple angles. A render is **not a print/cut file**; disclose measurements and source quality limitations.

## 11. Information design / infographic

Every quantity must come from a verified source or be clearly labeled example data. Use units, meaningful axes, legible text, minimal chartjunk and a truthful comparison basis. Do not add decorative donut charts just to make a branding board look credible.

## 12. Deliverables versus presentation

A social post should export the post, not a phone screenshot; a logo should export artwork, not a row of mockups; a vehicle design should preserve actual vehicle category; a poster should not require a UI to hold it together. Presentation mockups are separate outputs, only when requested.
