# Identity systems, logos and branding craft

Brand identity is a coherent visual behavior, not a page packed with logo lockups, icons, mockups and swatches. A symbol may be useful; it is not mandatory. Preserve approved client identity unless redesign is explicitly authorized.

## 1. Strategy before mark-making

Understand the brand's offer, audience, category, positioning, desired personality and the distinction it needs. Find a **credible point of difference**, not invented claims. Review competitors when access and user intent allow, but never claim trademark clearance without appropriate checks.

## 2. Choose the mark architecture

- Wordmark: brand name's letterform carries recognition.
- Monogram / lettermark: initials when naming or space constraints justify it.
- Symbol: meaningful, recognizable and distinguishable on its own.
- Combination: flexible symbol + logotype system for appropriate use cases.
- Emblem: integrated heritage/community context, often less flexible small.

Logo success is not measured by the number of hidden meanings. A single perceptually memorable feature may be enough. Reject arbitrary swooshes, generic map pins, stock arrows, orbiting circles, gradients masking weak structure and accidental lookalikes.

## 3. Wordmark construction test

Inspect coherent stroke weighting, x-height, ascenders/descenders, counters, terminals, inner spacing, optical kerning, overall word silhouette and small-size clarity. Modify letterforms systematically, not by adding disconnected decorations to one letter. A logo that cannot stand as a one-color mark often depends too heavily on effects.

## 4. Distinctiveness and plausibility

Ask whether the mark could identify any random delivery, fashion or fintech company. If yes, refine concept or forms. Test black on white, reversed white on dark, 24–48 px digital sizes and full-size signage as appropriate. Avoid claiming trademark originality without clearance.

## 5. Identity expansion sequence

1. Establish logo selection and master artwork.
2. Define wordmark/symbol relationships and clear space.
3. Assign palette roles with actual values and allowable substitutions.
4. Define typography roles and composition language.
5. Develop **one or two** meaningful graphics tied to identity; do not introduce several unrelated motifs.
6. Validate practical applications: real-world scale, substrate, production method, placement and visual fidelity.
7. Present a restrained, prioritized set of applications appropriate to brief; don't default to a huge moodboard.

## 6. Locking approved assets

Once approved, the logo is an **asset**, not a prompt. Preserve its letter contours and spacing. Use SVG/AI/EPS/approved transparent raster when available; composite rather than generate new approximations. A raster traced from a low-resolution screenshot is only a provisional reconstruction. In a photographed mockup, check individual logo instances for distortion, truncation or unwanted relettering.

## 7. Brand system is not creative imprisonment

Fixed identity elements can support many campaign directions: cinematic product scene, minimal typography, narrative documentary, conceptual metaphor, diagram, artful collage. Brand recognition comes from invariant rules and consistent voice, not mandatory repetition of one layout.

## 8. Brand-board quality gate

No invented statistics, fictional testimonials, fabricated partnerships, fake ratings, gratuitous app screens or irrelevant vehicles. The board should make the **design system** clear, not create a false impression of a deployed business. Label conceptual applications as concepts when necessary.
