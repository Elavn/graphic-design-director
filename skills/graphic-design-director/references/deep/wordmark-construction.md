# Wordmark construction — from a font to a proprietary-looking mark

Do not call a default font with a tiny notch a custom logo. At the same time, a genuinely original mark must remain readable and perform at small scale. The design is a shape system, not a decorated font.

## A serious wordmark study

1. Start with meaning, voice and phonetic rhythm before drawing letterforms.
2. Design or meaningfully modify at least **two interacting glyph relationships** when originality is requested: shared rhythm, terminal grammar, stroke contrast, optical joint, counters, connectors, or letter-specific structure. This is a working heuristic, not a numerical guarantee of novelty.
3. Construct a master vector from editable paths when tools permit; every displayed version must reuse the **same master**, not a retyped approximation.
4. Examine optical space around repeated shapes, awkward diagonals and joins. Correct spacing in context, not with equal numerical intervals.
5. Test black on white, reversed values, small-scale reading, low-resolution reproduction and monochrome printing.
6. If a repair, connection or transformation is the idea, make it **legible** as part of the wordmark without becoming an unrelated symbol or damaging readability.
7. Document how the design differs from the starter font or lettering reference; record what was redrawn. Glyph paths alone prove vector construction, not legal originality.

## Rejection triggers

- Wordmark could be recreated with one stock typeface and a tiny cut without meaningful loss.
- "Unique" changes are too small to read at real application sizes.
- Reverse sample changes letter proportions or tracking.
- Counter shapes close at small size or become visually identical to different letters.
- Unexplained swooshes, arrow P icons, unrelated gradients, and added standalone badges appear without the brief requesting them.

**Review evidence:** original vector master, glyph construction, side-by-side silhouette at a small target size, black/reverse overlay consistency, reference/font provenance and human distinctiveness review. Distinctiveness and trademark availability are not established by code checks.
