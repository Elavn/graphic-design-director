# Three user-supplied reference studies — art-direction lessons, not copy templates

These notes study three images supplied in the conversation on 2026-11-17. The images are **not bundled**, because public reuse rights and ownership were not established. In INSPIRE/CREATE, do not copy recognizable layouts, identity, marks, people or credited works; use the mechanisms below to invent an original approach. In an explicit, authorized REPLICATE/ADAPT request, preserve the user-provided source geometry and make only the requested content/brand edits.

## Study 1 — Typographic editorial collage / lively community graphic

**Observed:** White graph-paper ground with a precise repeated grid; oversized black lettering mixing high-contrast serif with dense bold sans; lines of type function as the main visual structure. A translucent-looking pastel pink horizontal strip threads through the major word; small green and cyan speech-label chips sit on top of typography; tiny stickers and hand/cursor-like interface motifs make the experience feel like an active creative community. The top navigation and generous white margin make it feel like a digital brand landing page rather than a standard flyer.

**Why it works:** (1) Immediate typographic dominance, (2) tension between expressive display typography and useful UI-like labels, (3) playful overlays that all serve a community/social theme, (4) repeated grid giving control to an otherwise energetic layout, (5) purposeful scale contrast from giant headline to small annotations.

**Learn:** Try a type-as-composition route: choose one phrase, build a tight expressive typographic silhouette, add only a few meaningfully related interactive or editorial annotations, and retain readable text. Mix serif and sans *by role*, not for arbitrary contrast. A grid can underpin a visually disruptive arrangement.

**Avoid:** Copying the exact headline, graph-paper-pink-strip-cherry combination, title overlap, position of callouts, visual stickers, or logo. Avoid overlays hiding the vital content. Avoid making every project resemble a UI screenshot.

**Original application test:** For a community radio launch, create typography shaped as a waveform (not the referenced title), with three utility tags corresponding to actual listening categories and an accurately spaced schedule module. Use a different color/material language.

## Study 2 — Conceptual poster / fragile last connection

**Observed:** A light sky-blue field with enormous negative space. A rope/fraying-fiber-like scene carries tiny figures in a surreal composite, meeting at a fragile stretched junction. Black quiet sans typography sits beneath the central action; one crucial numerical idea is highlighted in green. A restrained navigation row and small footer labels add editorial precision. The symbolic image delivers the story before the copy explains it.

**Why it works:** (1) One metaphor is immediately understandable, (2) believable photography/textures anchor an impossible scene, (3) visual tension concentrated in a small focal region against an open field, (4) color accent points directly to the message's critical fact, (5) typography supports rather than competes with the metaphor.

**Learn:** Develop a *visual proposition*: `message → emotional tension → familiar physical analogy → impossible but legible composition → concise sentence`. Give the focal action ample space. Keep materials, shadows and physical scale credible except for the deliberately surreal feature.

**Avoid:** Copying the rope, small figures, text, exact statistic, cropped diagonal, or specific creative arrangement. Don't use unverifiable percentages in genuine ads. A metaphor should never depend on tiny details invisible on phones.

**Original application test:** For a water-conservation concept, make a single droplet physically support an oversized dry leaf with realistic light, and place the factual call to action in a quiet lower third. Do not invent water statistics.

## Study 3 — Playful conceptual advertising / tactile product metaphor

**Observed:** Saturated cornflower-blue field, confident empty space, a large grayscale photographed hand and shiny spoon with small colorful software-icon-like objects. A tilted comic speech bubble, an organic white cutout shape, and a restrained typographic footer introduce cheerful, almost cut-paper advertising energy. The material contrast is central: grayscale photo, saturated field, small polished icons and flat shapes.

**Why it works:** (1) One witty and concrete action becomes the visual hook, (2) surprising materials and scale make the scene memorable, (3) expressive but highly limited palette, (4) tight separation between metaphor and small metadata, (5) careful shadows and object scale make a collage feel tactile.

**Learn:** For a playful campaign, find an action verb in the promise and turn it into a visual analogy. Use one hero object, one controlled visual surprise, and a concise copy line. Combine realistic photo with graphic speech or cut-paper elements only if they reinforce the concept.

**Avoid:** Reproducing the hand, spoon, exact blue, brand icons, comic title, or positions. Real logos/icons need permission/brand compliance. A surreal image can be delightful without becoming random clipart.

**Original application test:** For a creative-writing app, show a real typewriter feeding out a ribbon of colorful hand-drawn story-world shapes; use restrained inky violet and paper-white with a small yellow signal accent. Ensure it is distinct from the reference composition.

## Reference-derived quality targets (all three)

1. A recognizable **idea**, not just a mood.
2. A **hero**: oversized type *or* compelling physical metaphor, never six equal focal elements.
3. **Contrast of scale and material**: bold vs tiny; flat vs tactile; image vs clear type.
4. **Aggressive restraint**: 1–2 accent treatments that serve the core idea.
5. **Functional detail**: small UI labels/footers only when they represent real useful information; don't invent site navigation on posters.
6. **Audience-first hierarchy**: thumbnail reads; full-size rewards with detail.
7. **Originality safeguard**: in INSPIRE/CREATE, transfer design logic instead of recognizable composition; in REPLICATE/ADAPT, follow the authorized source geometry.

## Critique prompts based on these studies

- Can the graphic be described in one striking sentence *without* mentioning a trendy effect?
- Is the message embodied by the dominant object/typography rather than explained by decoration?
- Is the empty space doing a job, or is the composition merely underfilled?
- Could the metaphor survive at 25% scale?
- Are the micro-labels accurate, readable at intended size and necessary?
- Does the piece feel designed for this brand, rather than assembled from transferable style clichés?

## 2026-11-18 live calibration: distinguish mechanism from exact visual identity

Two trial image generations visually echoed the supplied first reference, including a lookalike masthead/annotated collage structure, and ignored the independently agreed fictional brand and headline. Those are **hard fails**, even where image craft and typographic contrast were strong. A deterministic editorial vector poster kept correct words and the intended folding-paper idea but showed less tactile depth than the references. The resulting rule is to lock brief-critical invariants, avoid source-image composition conditioning for new brands where possible, and separate generated hero assets from exact typesetting.
