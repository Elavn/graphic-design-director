# Concept engine: turn a brief into distinctive designed images

## Five-pass ideation
1. **Meaning:** Rewrite the brief as a plain claim: `People should understand/feel/do ____.` Check supplied facts.
2. **Concrete hook:** Write 5 ordinary nouns and 5 verbs from the brand, audience or message. Avoid safe symbols (lightbulbs, puzzle pieces, handshake, rocket) unless the product truly requires them.
3. **Mechanism:** Explore at least three *different* rhetorical devices where appropriate: (a) type becomes image, (b) visual metaphor or physical impossibility, (c) meaningful contrast in scale/material, (d) one-scene narrative, (e) inversion/negative space, (f) a surprising but truthful data visualization.
4. **Sketch in words:** For each route state focal object/shape, viewer eye path, text zone, palette logic, image treatment, and one identifying difference. Distinct routes must change **structure and concept**, not just colors.
5. **Select:** Prefer strongest on message fit, novelty without confusion, practical execution, clarity at thumbnail, asset feasibility and cultural sensitivity.

## Composition archetypes: rotate, do not overuse
- **Type architecture:** 70–85% of visual energy in deliberate expressive typography, meaningful chips/labels for secondary details, strict underlying grid.
- **Hero metaphor:** one believable surreal image with heavy negative space, quiet copy, one signal-color semantic accent.
- **Tactile visual joke:** a familiar physical action interpreted as an unexpected but logical product behavior; cutout/real photo hybrid, limited illustrative support.
- **Documentary contrast:** authentic photo + strong typographic statement with non-fake editorial metadata.
- **Information spectacle:** genuinely useful statistics presented with elegant, original graphic explanation, no invented numbers.

## Required concept brief before image rendering
- **Thesis:** what the design says in one short line.
- **Hero element:** one thing viewed first, with precise meaning.
- **Treatment:** photo, photomontage, illustration, 3D, typography, or mixed media.
- **Structure:** grid, margins, crop, text-safe areas, ratio, whitespace plan.
- **Roles:** display, body, utility, accent and any key figure.
- **Proof of difference:** why this could not be swapped into an unrelated campaign.
- **Asset/copyright check:** third-party rights; factual claims and trademarks.

## Image-generation / rendering workflow
- If capable of generating images: create a concept-first hero with intentional crop, lens, material, lighting, shadow, style and negative space. Request *no typography within the generated image* where actual editable typesetting is possible, then typeset headlines and details using SVG, CSS, canvas, Illustrator, Canva or other appropriate design tool.
- If only raster image generation is available: request precise copy and verify every glyph, date and brand name in the result. Re-render or separately typeset inaccurate lettering. Do not call an image final if the copy is wrong.
- If exact brand assets/logos are supplied, place original approved assets using a tool capable of preserving fidelity; don't hallucinate a lookalike.
- If only text tools exist: supply detailed render spec, prompt, hierarchy and coordinates, and state that no visual file was produced.
- Respect requested image ratio and safe areas; social-story UI overlap zones differ by platform and should be tested on the target app.

## Competing motivations
- **High concept + low readability:** keep surprising hero, relocate copy to strong contrast panel.
- **High clarity + generic:** change core metaphor or typographic mechanism, not merely add effects.
- **High polish + low brand fit:** remove or replace style until it sounds/looks like this brand's voice.
- **Good visual + fake facts:** fail immediately; make placeholder explicit or source facts.
