# Brand identity, logo systems, and recognizable marks

**Conceptual influences:** Per Mollerup, Jens Müller, Alina Wheeler, Paul Rand.

## Brand strategy first
Define category, audience, promise, personality, differentiator, market context and touchpoints. Distinguish temporary campaign style from enduring identity. A logo is only one element of a coherent visual system.

## Existing client identity is not a logo concept
If the client supplies an existing identity, do not redesign it, even subtly. Before making brand applications, follow [brand-asset-fidelity.md](brand-asset-fidelity.md). AI redraws of established brand marks fail fidelity even when aesthetically appealing. Use source artwork directly wherever possible.

## Logo concept method
1. Write the brand's essential idea in one sentence. Gather 10 relevant words or associations; avoid the first obvious visual cliché.
2. Explore multiple **structural** routes: wordmark, lettermark, monogram, symbol, combination, typographic construction, emblem.
3. Draw simple black-and-white silhouette concepts; prioritize recognition and meaning before effects.
4. Evaluate distinctiveness, balance, negative space, and applicability to signage, mobile icon, embroidery, packaging and social avatar.
5. Build horizontal, stacked and single-color alternatives only where genuinely useful; set clear space and minimum size.
6. Define the rest of the system: type roles, palette roles, grid/margins, imagery, illustrations, graphic motifs, and copy tone.
7. Check against recognizable competitor marks and relevant trademark risks. Do not promise legal clearance.

## Modern identity treatments
- **Typographic signature:** custom-feeling wordmark with a distinctive letter relationship, complemented by minimal utility type.
- **Constructed geometry:** a small reusable symbol vocabulary that carries through layouts and motion.
- **Expressive flexibility:** variable brand system with consistent base shapes and changing pattern/color modules.
- **Warm modern craft:** tactility, organic edges or local craft references when genuinely grounded in the brand.
- **Refined restraint:** expressive proportion and thoughtful whitespace instead of gratuitous gold gradients.

## Logo scorecard
- Recognizable at 24–32px? Works one-color? Distinguishable from competitors? Concept fits brand? Reproducible? Typeface licensed/usable? Consistent with system? Cultural associations considered?

## Reject and repair
- **Reject** universal swoosh/planet/abstract loop with no brand connection. **Repair:** derive form from authentic story or name.
- **Reject** complex gradients hiding weak contours. **Repair:** simplify and test one-color.
- **Reject** a brand guide containing only logo and colors. **Repair:** specify typography, imagery, grid, iconography and examples.
- **Reject** imitation of a famous mark or a designer's specific work. **Repair:** seek a differentiated visual route.

## “What these books would reject” heuristic
A logo can look attractive and still fail at identity. Memorability, appropriateness and consistent use matter more than stylistic effects.
