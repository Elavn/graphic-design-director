# Worked art-direction examples and negative examples

These are original examples to teach decisions, not mockups or claims of completed graphics. Colors, fonts and compositions are proposals that should be checked in a rendered design.

## Example A — Premium fragrance launch: editorial luxury
**Brief:** launch a niche fragrance, memorable and restrained.
**Weak generic approach:** gold gradient with giant perfume bottle, swirling smoke, sparkles, small wordmark and generic “discover luxury” headline.
**Strong direction:** **“The space after the scent.”** One intimate still-life with deep quiet shadow; narrow warm-paper typography panel; a single sensual serif headline; micro-detail copy in calm sans; negative space carries the sophistication.
**System:** 2-column asymmetrical structure; palette ink #121212, parchment #F1EDE4, antique olive #6D674C; headline large, details understated but readable.
**Why it works:** distinctive editorial mood, material credibility and one clear visual signature instead of “luxury effects.”
**Stress test:** name, product and CTA remain visible in 1080×1350 format.

## Example B — Community cultural festival: grounded contemporary heritage
**Brief:** celebrate community, music and food; need flyer + square + story.
**Weak generic approach:** generic “tribal” motifs, several unsupported cultural symbols, random bright shades, too many portraits and an unreadable footer.
**Strong direction:** **“One street, many rhythms.”** Use supplied authentic photography or vetted local illustration of the actual event context. Large rhythmic headline with a modular grid; repeat a real visual motif sourced from event identity; warm palette and strong type; place date/location in a dedicated clear info panel.
**System:** warm bone, night ink, one festival accent chosen from real supplied assets; editorial display paired with open sans body; relevant imagery rather than invented symbols.
**Why it works:** human specificity and cultural credibility without stereotypes.
**Stress test:** all festival details survive thumbnail; story version uses a full-height reflow, no illegible flyer shrink.

## Example C — Design-conference poster: neo-Swiss + kinetic type
**Brief:** attract creative professionals to a conference.
**Weak generic approach:** glass cards and gradients, a floating 3D blob, copy all center-aligned.
**Strong direction:** **“Ideas refuse the box.”** Precise modular 6-column grid with one huge word stepping beyond an alignment boundary; sharp black/off-white field and a single acid accent; speaker/date module aligned and highly readable.
**System:** structured grotesk, utility labels, clear typography-led focal point.
**Why it works:** tension between structure and disruption illustrates the idea.
**Stress test:** removing the acid accent still leaves the hierarchy intact.

## Example D — Youth music poster: expressive collage
**Brief:** announce a one-night experimental music performance.
**Weak generic approach:** overlaid flames, several neon glows, small date, hard-to-read distressed type.
**Strong direction:** **“Sound leaves marks.”** One enlarged contour of a sound-wave-like typographic form and restrained cut-paper overlays; imperfect edges but strict information alignment; date/time/venue in a solid high-contrast strip.
**System:** black, warm paper, vivid orange accent; one bold display treatment, quiet supporting text.
**Why it works:** expressive without sacrificing practical information.

## Example E — Professional-services identity: clean corporate, not bland
**Brief:** boutique financial advisory, confidence and differentiation.
**Weak generic approach:** navy gradient, stock handshake photograph, generic rising-arrow icon.
**Strong direction:** **“Clarity through complexity.”** A subtle typographic relationship becomes a signature identity device; spare columns; beautiful number setting; restrained architectural photography; one bright but controlled accent.
**System:** strong functional sans family, dark blue-black and warm neutral with one cobalt signal; clear rules for reports and social posts.
**Why it works:** personality comes from precise typography and information clarity, not stock financial symbolism.

## Example F — Surreal product campaign: one impossible idea
**Brief:** lightweight travel bottle.
**Weak generic approach:** random chrome spheres and swirling liquid around the product.
**Strong direction:** **“The bottle that carries less.”** One credible product photograph on a giant folded paper wing that visually exaggerates lightness; one well-behaved shadow and precise CTA. Keep actual brand/product attributes true.
**System:** mostly neutral studio palette, one accent, editorial display plus utility text.
**Why it works:** surrealism demonstrates the product proposition and is legible at a glance.

## Example G — How to critique an existing design
**Observation:** Event title and date have equal weight, flower textures fight with five fonts, contact text is too close to trim.
**Revise:** (1) give title 2× visual mass of date, (2) limit display treatment to one face, (3) simplify flowers to a coherent two-zone motif and match palette, (4) move contact text inside safe area, (5) inspect at phone preview and print size.
**Do not:** respond only “make it pop” or “use more modern fonts.”

## A reusable mini-prompt for the skill
“Design a 1080×1350 social announcement for [event]. Audience: [who]. Message: [what]. Must include exactly [facts]. Style preference: [adjectives]. Create two concept routes with distinct visual signatures. Choose the stronger route using message fit, hierarchy, type craft and originality. Specify grid, palette with hex roles, typesetting hierarchy, image direction and adaptable story version. Critique and revise; do not invent missing facts or generic decorative objects.”
